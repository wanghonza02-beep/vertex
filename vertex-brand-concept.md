# Vertex — Brand & Design Concept

## 1. Name

**Vertex**

Short, one-word, easy to pronounce internationally. "Vertex" = the apex, the highest point — the exact spot where two curves meet. It captures the brand's core tension: the point where the soul of a classic car meets the performance of a modern one.

---

## 2. Why We Exist (Brand Philosophy)

Perfection isn't found in the newest technology, and it isn't found in old-world nostalgia either — it's found at the vertex, the point where both meet.

Modern hypercars have become sterile and digitalized. They are fast, but interchangeable — even a limited-run supercar is still one of hundreds of nearly identical VINs. That's not individuality, it's just a more expensive uniform.

The automotive icons of the past — air-cooled Porsches, classic Mercedes-Benz roadsters, vintage Lamborghinis — carry raw soul and timeless design, but are held back by outdated performance and technology.

We don't take that soul away. We elevate it.

We take the most iconic chassis from the past century, strip them to bare metal, and rebuild them using the finest modern engineering available today — injecting modern horsepower, razor-sharp handling, bespoke hand-stitched interiors, and discreet modern tech into a legendary silhouette. The result is a car that feels alive in a way no modern hypercar can, with the reliability and performance a modern owner expects.

## 3. Mission

We take the most iconic chassis in automotive history and rebuild them at the exact point where character meets engineering — without compromising either.

## 4. Core Values

1. **Obsession with Detail** — 1,000+ hours per build, no shortcuts.
2. **Defiance of Sterility** — a conscious rejection of the uniform, digitalized design language of modern cars.
3. **Exclusivity as Standard** — no mass production; limited, master-crafted pieces only.
4. **Craft Over Automation** — hand work wherever a machine falls short.
5. **Uniqueness, Not Just Rarity** — new hypercars are limited, but identical to each other. Every Vertex build is one of a kind on the planet — not because we call it "limited edition," but because it physically cannot exist twice. It is built from one specific piece of history and one owner's own choices — color, leather, the sound of its exhaust.

---

## 5. Voice & Tone

**Positioning: Luxurious and exclusive.** Vertex speaks the way genuine expertise speaks — with restraint, not noise.

- **Confident, not boastful.** State facts (hours invested, materials, engineering) and let them carry the weight — no exclamation marks, no "amazing"/"incredible."
- **Precise over hyped.** Prefer specific detail ("1,247 hours of hand-finishing") over vague superlatives ("the best in the world").
- **Quiet exclusivity.** Never chase mass appeal. Copy should feel like it was written for someone already inside the world of classic and performance cars, not someone who needs it explained from scratch.
- **Understated emotion.** Passion for the craft comes through in specificity and care, not in exclamation.

*Example line:* "We do not build cars. We resurrect legends." — this is the register: short, declarative, confident.

## 6. Business Model & Operations

### The Process
- **Donor Sourcing:** Acquisition of rare, highly sought-after classic donor vehicles (e.g. air-cooled Porsche 911s, classic Mercedes-Benz SL roadsters, vintage Lamborghini models).
- **The Rebuild (Restomodding):**
  - *Chassis & Body:* Full restoration, weight optimization, custom bespoke paint finishes, modern LED lighting integrated into classic housings.
  - *Interior:* Fully custom cabins — premium full-grain leathers, Alcantara, exposed carbon fiber, discreetly integrated modern amenities (audio, climate control).
  - *Powertrain & Mechanics:* Modernized high-output engines, upgraded high-performance braking, fully adjustable performance suspension, custom stainless-steel exhaust with a signature acoustic profile.
- **Monetization:** Given the extreme labor intensity (1,000+ hours per car) and top-tier components, vehicles are sold at ultra-premium, bespoke price points as exclusive collectors' items.

## 8. Design System

### Color Palette

| Token | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Vertex Black | `#0B0C0E` | Primary background |
| Graphite | `#1A1C1F` | Card / panel surfaces, elevated sections |
| Champagne Gold | `#C9A66B` | Primary accent — CTAs, key numbers, active states |
| Aged Brass | `#8C7248` | Secondary accent — hover states, muted highlights |
| Paper White | `#EDE8DD` | Primary text (warm off-white, not pure white — evokes old drawing paper) |
| Blueprint Line | `#2E3B47` | Technical overlay lines, dividers, dimension marks, borders |
| Corsa Red | `#C1272D` | Dynamic contrast accent — used sparingly for motion/scroll indicators, video-section hover states, and "rare/limited" tags (e.g. Ferrari donor builds) |

Rule: gold is used sparingly — for the single most important element on screen (a CTA, a headline stat), never as a general decoration color. Corsa Red is used even more sparingly than gold — reserved for motion, video overlays, and rare/limited signals, never as a base or primary color. Everything else stays in black/graphite/paper white so gold and red both keep their weight.

### Typography

Headlines use a deliberate **two-font split** that mirrors the brand's core tension — old-world soul vs. modern tech:

- **Luxury line (e.g. "Immortal Icons."):** *Italiana* — a thin, elegant, fashion-editorial serif. Used for the "soul"/heritage half of a headline. Paper White.
- **Tech line (e.g. "MODERN MUSCLE."):** *Orbitron*, heavy weight (900), uppercase, tracked out — a bold, geometric, futuristic display face with a retro-sci-fi character (think dashboard/HUD typography). Used for the "engineering"/modern half of a headline. Champagne Gold.
- **Body (paragraphs, UI copy):** *IBM Plex Sans* — clean, engineered, slightly technical without being cold. Carries the everyday reading text.
- **Labels / small data (specs labels, project codes, eyebrows):** *IBM Plex Mono* — used for supporting labels like "POWER OUTPUT," "0–100 KM/H," and project codes like "PROJECT #01 — 1989 PORSCHE 911."
- **Large spec numbers (signature element):** *JetBrains Mono*, bold, tight letter-spacing — used specifically for the oversized hero numbers (horsepower, 0–100 time, year) to read as sharp and engineered rather than soft.
- **Wordmark ("VERTEX"):** also set in *Italiana*, wide letter-spacing — keeps the brand name itself in the "luxury" register.

Rule: never mix the luxury and tech fonts within the same phrase — a headline should read as one clause in Italiana, then hand off cleanly to a second clause in Orbitron. This keeps the contrast intentional rather than noisy.

Type scale (base 16px):
- Hero display: 80–120px, Fraunces, tight line-height
- Section headline: 40–56px, Fraunces
- Body: 16–18px, IBM Plex Sans, 1.6 line-height
- Data / labels: 13–14px, IBM Plex Mono, uppercase, letter-spacing +0.05em

### Visual Language: Technical / Blueprint

- Thin `1px` Blueprint Line (`#2E3B47`) rules and dimension-style marks used as structural dividers — echoing engineering drawings, not decoration for its own sake.
- Sections can carry faint blueprint-style line work in the background (a barely-visible technical sketch of a chassis or panel line) — always subtle, never competing with content.
- Sharp corners throughout — **0px border-radius** on buttons, cards, images, inputs. No rounding anywhere. This is a deliberate contrast to typical "soft luxury" design and reinforces precision and engineering discipline.

### Signature Element: Blueprint Spec Numbers

The one element Vertex is remembered by: **oversized specification numbers, styled like dimension callouts on a technical drawing.**

- A large IBM Plex Mono number (e.g. `640 HP`, `3.2s`, `1989`) in Paper White or Champagne Gold.
- A thin gold leader line extending from the number, exactly like a dimension line on a blueprint.
- A small uppercase Plex Mono label beneath it (e.g. `POWER OUTPUT`, `0–100 KM/H`, `DONOR YEAR`).

This pattern repeats across the site — hero stats, vehicle detail pages, the Legacy Archive — so it becomes instantly recognizable as "a Vertex page" even without the logo present.

### Motion

Restrained and purposeful, not decorative:
- Blueprint lines "draw themselves" on scroll into view (short stroke animation), as if a technical drawing is being traced live.
- Spec numbers count up from 0 when scrolled into view.
- No hover bounce, no gratuitous parallax. Motion exists only to reinforce the technical/engineering feel.
